Why does hair turn gray? The biology behind lost pigment

Gray hair begins when follicles lose their pigment-making capacity. Explore the roles of melanocyte stem cells, genetics, age and oxidative stress.

By UpCare Health · Published October 10, 2026 · 3 min read

A silver strand can seem to appear overnight. The change, however, begins inside the follicle, where specialized cells make the melanin that colors each new hair.

Where does hair color come from?

Melanocytes in the hair bulb transfer pigment to a growing strand. The follicle also holds melanocyte stem cells that help replenish this pigment-producing system. Research reviews describe graying as a gradual failure of that system: stem cells can be lost, stop renewing normally or fail to become working melanocytes.1

Why does it happen at different ages?

Genetics and age have a major influence on when graying begins. Oxidative stress within the follicle may also damage pigment cells and the chemistry they rely on. Smoking and some health or nutritional conditions have been associated with premature graying, but a gray hair alone cannot identify a cause.2

Can you reverse it?

There is no established treatment that reliably restores pigment to ordinary age-related gray hair. When graying begins unusually early or arrives with other symptoms, a clinician can decide whether a medical or nutritional evaluation makes sense.

Most gray hair is a visible part of biology and time, not a warning that something is wrong.
