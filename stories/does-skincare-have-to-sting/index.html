Does skincare have to sting to work?

A burning serum is not proof of results. Learn what dermatologists say about stinging skincare, product directions and prescribed treatments.

By UpCare Health · Published October 10, 2026 · 2 min read

You apply a new serum. Your skin starts to sting. Perhaps, you think, that means the active ingredients are doing their job.

That sensation does not establish that a product is effective.

What do dermatologists say about stinging?

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends stopping products that sting or burn unless they were prescribed by a dermatologist. Some prescribed treatments can cause these sensations under medical supervision.1

A treatment’s expected effects should come from its instructions and your clinician’s guidance, rather than the intensity of a sensation.

Can more product mean more irritation?

Using more than directed can cause unwanted effects. The AAD also notes that using too many products, particularly multiple products aimed at signs of aging, can irritate skin.1

What should you look for instead?

Follow the directions and give the product its appropriate time to work. Results depend on the product and what it is intended to address.

If a prescribed treatment burns or stings, ask your dermatologist how to manage it. If an ordinary cosmetic product does, resist treating discomfort as a reason to keep applying it.

Your routine should have a clear purpose. Pain is not a measure of progress.
