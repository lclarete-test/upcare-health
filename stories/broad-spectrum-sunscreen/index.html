What does “broad-spectrum” sunscreen actually mean?

Understand broad-spectrum sunscreen, SPF and water resistance, plus why shade, clothing and reapplication matter for sun protection.

By UpCare Health · Published October 10, 2026 · 2 min read

You pick up a sunscreen and see three promises: SPF 50, broad-spectrum and water-resistant. They describe different parts of protection.

What does broad-spectrum mean?

Broad-spectrum sunscreen protects against both UVA and UVB rays. UVA contributes to premature skin aging. UVB is the main cause of sunburn. Both can contribute to skin cancer.1

Is SPF the whole story?

SPF describes protection against sunburn, primarily from UVB. The American Academy of Dermatology recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.1

Checking all three features gives you more information than choosing by the largest number alone.

Does water-resistant mean waterproof?

No. Follow the product’s directions for reapplication. When outdoors, the AAD recommends reapplying approximately every two hours, and after swimming or sweating as directed.1

What else helps protect your skin?

Sunscreen works alongside shade and protective clothing. It cannot fully protect you on its own.

Before your next walk or afternoon outside, read the label, cover exposed skin and plan for reapplication. The useful question is how the protection fits into the time you will spend outdoors.
