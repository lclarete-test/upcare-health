Can a greener route change how a walk feels?

A study compared green and urban walking routes. Explore what it found about mood, its limitations and an everyday way to try a different route.

By UpCare Health · Published October 10, 2026 · 2 min read

You have half an hour for a walk. One route follows busy streets. Another takes a detour through trees. The distance may be similar, but the experience can feel different.

What did researchers compare?

A 2022 experiment randomly assigned 150 university students to walking in a green setting, walking in an urban setting or a control group without exercise. Researchers assessed positive and negative feelings before and after the activity.1

Did the greener route make a difference?

The researchers found more favorable changes in emotional wellbeing with green walking than urban walking. The findings suggest that the setting may contribute to the experience, alongside the movement itself.1

What are the limits?

The participants were young students, and the experiment examined immediate changes. It cannot tell us that every park walk produces the same response, or establish a treatment for a mental health condition.

How could this fit into your day?

If you have access to a safe, comfortable green space, consider trying that route and noticing how you feel afterward. There is no score to achieve.

A tree-lined detour can be an invitation to explore your surroundings, without turning a walk into another performance goal.
